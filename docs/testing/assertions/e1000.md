# Assertions: Intel e1000 network controller rules

Source: NOLX M9 -- `ROADMAP.md` (Devices and drivers, Networking: e1000) and `crates/nolx-e1000-core`.
The rules cover the register map, the EEPROM read and MAC address, the receive address registers, the
control bits that enable the engines, the legacy receive and transmit descriptors, and the ring and
free-slot arithmetic. The kernel maps the registers, allocates the rings, and polls; that driver is a
later slice.

The register offsets (CTRL, STATUS, EERD, ICR, RCTL, TCTL, the RDBAL/TDBAL ring block at 0x2800 and
0x3800, RAL0/RAH0), the EERD start and done bits, the RCTL and TCTL bit positions, the legacy descriptor
layouts (16 bytes: buffer, length, command or status), the transmit command bits, the 128-byte ring
alignment, and the PCI IDs (8086:100E) are from the Intel 8254x developer's manual as recalled, not yet
checked against the document. Verify them before the rows are promoted.

Rows are pipe-delimited: `ID | specification reference | status | test`.

NIC-001 | NOLX M9 Register map and device identity: the control, status, interrupt, receive and transmit ring, and receive address registers sit at their offsets, and the device is the 82540EM (8086:100E) | tested-T1 | nolx-e1000-core tests::the_register_offsets_are_the_e1000_map
NIC-002 | NOLX M9 EEPROM and MAC address: an EEPROM read names its word and returns data only once done; the MAC address is three little-endian words, and the receive address registers hold it with the valid bit | tested-T1 | nolx-e1000-core tests::an_eeprom_read_names_its_word_and_returns_data_once_done, tests::the_mac_address_comes_from_three_little_endian_words, tests::the_receive_address_registers_hold_the_mac_with_the_valid_bit
